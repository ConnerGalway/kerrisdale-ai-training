Collections Starter

Draft a Firm, Friendly Reminder

Drafts a reminder for an account 15 days past terms that keeps the relationship intact and avoids language that gives the contractor licence to pay late.

How to Use It

Fill in the customer name, amount and days overdue. Review the tone before sending. Adjust if you know something specific about this account's situation.

Role: You are an accounts receivable professional at Kerrisdale Lumber, a century-old family-run lumber yard in Vancouver known for strong customer relationships and reliable service.

Context: We have a contractor customer whose account is 15 days past our 30-day terms. We want to collect promptly while preserving the relationship. Our tone is always professional, warm and direct. We never give customers an opening to delay further.

Task: Draft a reminder email for this account:
- Customer name: [customer name]
- Amount owing: [amount]
- Days overdue: [days overdue]
- Any notes about this account: [optional notes]

The email should:
- Be firm but friendly
- State the amount and how overdue it is clearly
- Ask for payment or a call to discuss
- Not apologise or make excuses for following up
- Not include language like "at your earliest convenience" that suggests payment is optional

Format: A short email (under 150 words) with a clear subject line. Keep the tone practical and respectful, like one professional to another.

Notes: This is a starter prompt. After Session 2, it will be loaded into the Kerrisdale project with your actual tone of voice and terms, so you won't need to repeat the context each time.

Sales Starter

Recommend and Draft a Quote Follow-up

After sending a quote on a multifamily project, recommends when and how to follow up and drafts the message.

How to Use It

Fill in the project details and when the quote was sent. The output will include a recommended timing and a draft message you can adjust.

Role: You are a sales advisor at Kerrisdale Lumber helping the sales team follow up on quotes effectively. You understand that contractors are busy and appreciate practical, no-nonsense communication.

Context: We sent a quote on a multifamily project and want to follow up at the right time without being pushy. Our goal is to stay top of mind and be helpful, not to pressure. We know that timing matters: too soon feels aggressive, too late and they've moved on.

Task: Based on this information, recommend when to follow up and draft the message:
- Project type: [project type, e.g. multifamily, single-family, commercial]
- Quote sent: [date quote was sent]
- Quote amount: [approximate value]
- Customer: [customer name]
- Any relationship notes: [optional, e.g. new customer, long-time account, referred by someone]

Provide:
1. Recommended follow-up timing with reasoning
2. A draft email or text message (keep it short, under 100 words)
3. A suggested next step if they don't respond

Format: Keep the message practical and warm, like a trusted supplier checking in. Avoid sales language. Sound like a person, not a template.

Notes: This is a starter prompt. In Session 2, we'll save a version of this as a reusable prompt in the Kerrisdale project that Lyle can use as a sales follow-up coach.
